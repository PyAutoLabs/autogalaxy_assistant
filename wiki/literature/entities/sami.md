# SAMI — the Sydney-AAO Multi-object Integral field spectrograph Galaxy Survey

## What it is

A multi-object integral-field survey on the Anglo-Australian Telescope. Its enabling idea is
instrumental: fused fibre "hexabundles" let many galaxies be observed with integral-field
units *simultaneously*, turning IFS from a one-galaxy-at-a-time technique into a survey
technique ([[sources-ifu-spectroscopy#croom-2012-the-sami-instrument]]).

The survey targets were drawn from the GAMA survey — four volume-limited samples selected on
a stellar-mass proxy, plus eight cluster fields added specifically to extend the range of
environments ([[sources-ifu-spectroscopy#bryant-2015-the-sami-galaxy-survey]]). The complete dataset was
published in the third and final data release
([[sources-ifu-spectroscopy#croom-2021-sami-data-release-3]]).

## Key facts

- **Telescope/instrument:** SAMI on the 3.9 m AAT, using fibre hexabundles.
- **Parent sample:** GAMA equatorial fields, plus cluster fields for environment coverage.
- **Final release:** SAMI Data Release 3 (MNRAS 505, 991), the complete survey.
- **Complementarity with [[manga]]:** similar science, different selection and hemisphere —
  SAMI reaches denser environments through its cluster fields.

## Why it matters for galaxy structure

SAMI is where the "is this photometric class the same as that kinematic class?" question
gets tested at survey scale. Higher-order stellar kinematics carry classification
information that neither a visual morphology nor a Sersic index reproduces
([[sources-ifu-spectroscopy#van-de-sande-2017-classification-through-higher-order-kinematics]]), which is a direct caution
against over-interpreting a structural fit as a statement about a galaxy's dynamics
([[morphology-classification]]).

Its environment coverage also makes it a natural place to ask whether structural parameters
depend on where a galaxy lives.
