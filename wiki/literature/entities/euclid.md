# Euclid

## What it is

An ESA space mission surveying a large fraction of the extragalactic sky with a
visible imager (VIS) and a near-infrared imager and spectrometer (NISP). Defined in the
Euclid Definition Study Report ([[sources-collaborations-and-surveys#laureijs-2011-the-euclid-mission-definition]])
and described as built in the mission overview
([[sources-collaborations-and-surveys#euclid-collaboration-2025-euclid-mission-overview]]).

## Key facts

- **Instruments:** VIS (a single broad optical band, optimised for image quality) and NISP
  (near-infrared photometry and slitless spectroscopy).
- **Scale:** a wide survey covering thousands of square degrees, plus deep fields.
- **Data releases:** the Quick Data Release 1 (Q1) delivered the first substantial public
  imaging, including the first Euclid visual morphology catalogue
  ([[sources-collaborations-and-surveys#walmsley-2025-the-q1-visual-morphology-catalogue]]).

## Why it matters for galaxy structure

Euclid is the regime shift that structural fitting has been preparing for: space-based
image quality over an area large enough that the number of resolved galaxies is measured in
the hundreds of millions. Two consequences:

1. **Morphology becomes a survey product.** The pre-launch forecasts used realistic
   simulated galaxies to establish what structural precision Euclid imaging supports
   ([[sources-collaborations-and-surveys#bretonniere-2022-galaxy-morphology-forecasts]]), and Q1
   delivered the first catalogue built on real data.
2. **Automation is not optional.** At Euclid's sample size, every structural measurement is
   made by a pipeline, so the systematics of profile choice, PSF treatment and sky estimation
   — which are per-galaxy annoyances today — become population-level biases
   ([[photometric-structural-fitting]], [[sky-subtraction-and-photometry]]).
