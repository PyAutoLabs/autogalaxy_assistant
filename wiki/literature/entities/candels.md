# CANDELS — the Cosmic Assembly Near-infrared Deep Extragalactic Legacy Survey

## What it is

The largest HST programme ever executed (902 orbits): a near-infrared imaging survey with
WFC3/IR, backed by ACS optical imaging, over five well-studied extragalactic fields —
GOODS-North, GOODS-South, UDS, EGS and [[cosmos-survey|COSMOS]]
([[sources-collaborations-and-surveys#grogin-2011-candels]]). It has a two-tier design: a
"deep" tier over a smaller area and a "wide" tier covering more sky at shallower depth.

Its companion paper documents the observations, data products and mosaics that the community
actually works from ([[sources-collaborations-and-surveys#koekemoer-2011-candels-data-products]]) —
CANDELS is as much a *data-products* project as an observing programme.

## Key facts

- **Instruments:** HST WFC3/IR (J and H bands, principally F125W and F160W) plus ACS optical.
- **Fields:** GOODS-N, GOODS-S, UDS, EGS, COSMOS — chosen for their existing multi-wavelength
  coverage.
- **Design goal:** the rest-frame optical morphology and stellar populations of galaxies at
  z ~ 1.5–3, plus supernova cosmology in the deep tier.
- **Legacy value:** homogeneous, publicly released mosaics and structural-parameter
  catalogues over five independent sightlines, which suppresses cosmic variance in any
  structural measurement made from them.

## Why it matters for galaxy structure

CANDELS is the reason "the size–mass relation at z ~ 2" is a quantitative statement rather
than an impression. WFC3/IR pushed resolved imaging into the rest-frame optical for the first
time out to z ~ 2–3, and the survey's structural-parameter catalogues — Sersic fits run
uniformly over every field — became the standard input for size evolution, bulge/disk work at
high redshift, and morphology classification training sets
([[high-z-galaxy-structure]], [[galaxy-scaling-relations]],
[[morphology-classification]]).

It is also the direct predecessor of what [[jwst]] surveys such as [[cosmos-web]] now do at
longer wavelengths: the same measurement, one more step into the rest-frame optical, at
higher redshift.
