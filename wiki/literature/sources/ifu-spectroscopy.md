# Sources: integral-field spectroscopy

Resolved spectroscopy is the check on what a photometric fit can and cannot tell you. A
bulge/disk decomposition is a statement about light; whether those components are
dynamically distinct is a question only kinematics answers.

## Bacon 2001 — SAURON

**Canonical BibTeX key:** `Bacon2001`
**Reference:** arXiv:astro-ph/0103451, MNRAS 326, 23 — "The SAURON project. I. The panoramic
integral-field spectrograph"
**Concepts:** [[early-type-galaxy-structure]]

**Supports:**
- The instrument and survey design that made panoramic integral-field spectroscopy of nearby
  early-type galaxies routine.

**Use when:**
- Citing the start of the modern IFS era for galaxy structure.

**Do not use for:**
- Survey results, which are in later SAURON papers.

## Cappellari 2011 — ATLAS3D survey definition

**Canonical BibTeX key:** `Cappellari2011`

**Concepts:** [[early-type-galaxy-structure]], [[morphology-classification]]

**Supports:**

**Use when:**

**Do not use for:**

## Emsellem 2011 — fast and slow rotators

**Canonical BibTeX key:** `Emsellem2011`
**Reference:** arXiv:1102.4444, MNRAS 414, 888 — "The ATLAS3D project - III. A census of the
stellar angular momentum within the effective radius of early-type galaxies: unveiling the
distribution of Fast and Slow Rotators"
**Concepts:** [[early-type-galaxy-structure]], [[morphology-classification]]

**Supports:**
- The kinematic classification of early-type galaxies into fast and slow rotators, based on
  the angular-momentum parameter measured within the effective radius.

**Use when:**
- Arguing that morphology and dynamics are different classification axes.

**Do not use for:**
- Photometric structural parameters.

## Sanchez 2012 — CALIFA

**Canonical BibTeX key:** `Sanchez2012`
**Reference:** arXiv:1111.0962, A&A 538, A8 — "CALIFA, the Calar Alto Legacy Integral Field
Area survey: I. Survey presentation"
**Concepts:** [[disk-galaxy-structure]], [[stellar-mass-estimates]]

**Supports:**
- An integral-field survey of several hundred nearby galaxies spanning the full Hubble
  sequence, with coverage extending to large galactocentric radii.

**Use when:**
- You need resolved spectroscopy across morphological types, not just early types.

**Do not use for:**
- Large-sample statistics — MaNGA is an order of magnitude bigger.
